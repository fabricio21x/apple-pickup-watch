# Product

## Register

product

## Users
One person: the owner, who runs the monitor on their own always-on Mac and is its only user. They usually open the page on their phone right after a Telegram stock alert, often on the move or in daylight, and have seconds to decide where to pick up before the stock sells out. Less often they glance at it on the Mac to check that the monitor is still running.

## Product Purpose
Pickup watch tracks in-store pickup stock for exact iPhone configurations at the Apple Stores near one zip code, alerts on Telegram the moment one comes into stock, and gets the owner to Apple's checkout for it. It succeeds when a real stock event turns into a reservation before the units are gone.

## Brand Personality
Fast, calm, precise. It speaks plainly and briefly, like a departure board: one clear state per item, no hype, no urgency theatre. The emotional goal is trust: the owner believes what it shows, including when it shows nothing.

## Anti-references
- The first version's stock board: a flat list with the color name repeated on every row, grey badges, every store given equal weight, and long scrolling to find the few that matter.
- Apple's marketing pages: huge product photography and hero type. Beautiful, but slow to scan and wrong for a tool.

## Design Principles
- Act first, read later: what can be acted on (a store with stock, a Buy button) leads; everything else recedes or collapses.
- Color always means something: availability or a phone's real finish, never decoration.
- Quiet when nothing changed: no motion or emphasis without a change in state.
- Freshness is visible: the last check and the monitor's health are always in view, so silence can be trusted.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG AA contrast, keyboard reachable, labelled controls, visible focus. Availability is always stated in words (Today, Tomorrow, Unavailable, Not checked), never by color alone. No known specific needs beyond that.
